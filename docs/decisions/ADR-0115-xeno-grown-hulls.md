# ADR-0115 — Xeno grown hulls: per-cell depth and surface nets

**Status:** Accepted (2026-10-14, QA-001, owner request "add in the alien looking ship and its functionality (it uses a different method of rendering than normal blocks, read about it in the design and lookbook)")

## Context
DESIGN-GUIDE §7 (Binding): "Xeno ships (the aliens) are grown, not built. The cells hold depth values and are meshed smooth by surface nets, so damage comes out organic, with glowing flesh inside." The look book's ALIENS tab describes the technique:
- same grid as every other ship;
- each alien cell stores one small number, how deep inside the shell it is;
- a single-pass surface-nets mesher builds a smooth shell, in the same cost class as the block mesher;
- damage just removes cells, so craters come out organic;
- spines, sacs, eyes and tendrils are the alien "blocks" on top;
- the vein pattern is a shader, so any colourway is a palette swap.

The renders came from `ArtSource/LookDev/ld_aliens.py` (signed-distance primitives sampled on the 0.5 m grid). The game had no smooth meshing, and the campaign's xeno-faction encounters spawned human blueprints as placeholders.

## Decision
1. **Data.** `BlockShape.Xeno = 5` is a structural shape. A Xeno cell keeps its depth below the grown surface in `Cell.Flags` bits 2..7 (`XenoDepth`, 0..63 sixteenths of a cell). VOXS already persists flags, so blueprints, saves and damage keep it. Physics, damage, raycasts and the editor treat the cell as a full cube; only rendering differs.
2. **Snapshot.** A Xeno cell has no orientation, so its snapshot orient field carries the depth in eighths of a cell. Xeno cells occlude neighbouring block faces but emit none of their own.
3. **Surface nets** (`BrickMesher.MeshXeno`, LOD0 at stride 1 and LOD1 at stride 2):
   - samples at cell centres: air +0.5, tissue −(depth + 1/16), any other solid −0.5;
   - functional cells inside a brick that holds tissue count as tissue, so organs leave no holes;
   - one vertex per sign-changing dual cube, at the mean of its edge crossings, with a trilinear-gradient normal;
   - a quad only where the inside end of the edge is tissue, owned by the brick holding the edge's lower sample;
   - vertices are emitted lazily, so a brick never uploads orphan geometry.
4. **Vertex format.** Smooth vertices set flag bit 5 (`PackedBrickVertex.PackSmooth`) and carry:
   - positions in eighths of a cell (offset −0.5);
   - an octahedral normal in the normal byte and the spare top byte of `Lo`;
   - the shallowest tissue depth (quarter cells, 0..15) in the AO bits and flag bits 6–7.
   Both brick shaders decode both encodings through one function.
5. **Shading** (`XenoSurface.hlsl`, layer style 5), in the NOCTURNE colourway Obsidian & acid:
   - glossy obsidian chitin;
   - a ridged acid-vein network that fades, rather than widens, below a pixel;
   - dark grooves;
   - living flesh that glows wherever the surface cuts tissue deeper than the grown shell (a wound or a sac).
   Organ props (heart, spitter eyes, fins) use the same shading with an organ glow amount.
6. **Content** is generated by code (`Game/Content/Build Xeno Content`, own work):
   - blocks: tissue, sac (maximum depth, so it bulges and glows), heart (core), spitter (`wpn_xeno_acid`) and fin (thruster), with procedural organ meshes;
   - ships: HUSK (2.8k cells) and BROODMOTHER (16k cells), ported from the look book designs;
   - encounters: the xeno-faction encounters (probe tutorial, glass worms, jungle beasts, solar parasite) now spawn them;
   - playground: pad 15 spawns a wave.

## Amendment (QA-001, 2026-10-15)
Owner, verbatim: "art approval, yes aliens look good, i couldn't break their bodies though its like theyre invincible".
- **Wounds open.** Depth is fixed at spawn, so tissue exposed by damage kept its old depth and the shell regrew over craters: a crater dug to y = 12 meshed its floor at 12.375. The mesher now treats a tissue cell deeper than 1.2 cells that touches air as a wound. A pristine cell touching air is at most about one cell deep, because the field is a distance. Its geometry samples a shallow depth (0.2), so the floor follows the removed cells (11.875); its shading keeps the true depth, so the wound still glows as flesh. Interior depth is capped one code below the maximum (3.75 cells), so only sacs carry the maximum and keep their bulge.
- **Flesh is soft.** Grown hulls are solid: a HUSK (2,843 cells) against a fighter (1,005 mostly thin cells). Tissue drops from 80 to 30 HP and sacs from 40 to 15. A HUSK then holds about 85k HP, close to a fighter's 100k, and the BROODMOTHER (about 490k) stays a boss.

## Consequences
- The opening chapter's first alien contact is three HUSKs instead of human frigates.
- A Xeno brick costs one extra sampling pass, and only when the brick holds tissue. Human bricks exit after one scan of the snapshot.
- Colliders stay cubic, so shots and ramming meet the cell, not the smooth shell. That is at most half a cell of error.
- GRAVID, LAMPREY and CRINOID are later content. The technique and the builder already support them.
- Tendrils and very thin spines below half a cell vanish. Designs thicken them.

## Alternatives considered
- **Separate managed meshes per alien grid.** That doubles the rendering paths, culling and LOD, and loses the brick pool's remesh-on-damage.
- **Binary occupancy only (no depth).** Surface nets over a 0/1 field gives chamfered blobs, and wounds cannot glow by depth.
- **Pre-made alien prop meshes.** That gives no organic damage, against DESIGN-GUIDE §7.
