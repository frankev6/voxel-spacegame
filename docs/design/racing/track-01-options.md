# First race track: layout options (RACE-002)

Three options for the first track, plus the mechanics they are built from. All are true-scale top-down maps on the flight plane (SPEC-92 decision 3). Images and coordinates are generated by `ArtSource/Racing/track_layouts.py`; the coordinates RACE-004 needs are in [track_options.json](track_options.json).

**Every mechanic is a general world system.** The owner asked for foundations that work in exploration, not only in races. The race tracks are just the first place they are put together. Each card on the mechanics sheet says what the mechanic does while exploring the galaxy first, then on a track, then what it is built from.

## Option A: Derelict Run

![Option A](img/track_option_a.png)

A 1 km dead dreadnought lies across the north-east corner. The lap goes around it, along its flank under its guns, or straight through its hangar. The bulkheads there have to be shot open, and the blast door's switch is on the gun deck.

| Route | Clean lap | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Outer line | 74 s | Crane sweepers; nothing shoots at you |
| Gun deck | 68 s | Fastest on lap 1; from lap 2 three turrets target the leader |
| Hangar run | 69 s | Shut until someone shoots the bulkheads; the door can trap you |

## Option B: Moon Slingshot

![Option B](img/track_option_b.png)

A figure eight. The east lobe orbits a moon's gravity well:
- **high orbit:** wide, but sunlit and hit by flares;
- **low orbit:** narrow and shadowed, with debris orbiting between the two lines.

The west lobe circles a station whose arms sweep the track, and a pair of lane-gate portals skips the arms. Each portal jams for 6 s after use. The two lobes meet at a level crossing with repulsor mines.

| Route | Clean lap | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| High orbit | 62 s | Flares on the far side |
| Low orbit | 59 s | Narrow, debris, the moon is a wall |
| Portal + high orbit | 50 s | Jammed 6 s after each use: the leader denies it |
| Portal + low orbit | 47 s | The fastest lap, and the most easily denied |

## Option C: Foundry Gauntlet

![Option C](img/track_option_c.png)

An ore foundry has three set pieces:
- a docked freighter that test-fires its engines across the start straight;
- smelter heat vents;
- an ore conveyor carrying rocks across the S-bend.

After a grapple hairpin the track forks into three lanes. The boost lane runs under a turret platform, and the platform holds the switch for the lane's exit door. The barricade lane is narrow and walled. The safe lane carries the energy canisters. All three merge at an EMP minefield.

| Route | Clean lap | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| Safe lane | 71 s | Longest; canisters refill boost |
| Boost lane | 69 s | Four turrets; the exit door can slam |
| Barricade lane | 69 s | Two barricades; one ship blocks it |

## How the lap times are made
- **Flight limits:** a clean line with production flight limits: cap 120 m/s, boost 192 m/s, about 25 m/s² forward, 20 m/s² braking and sideways grip. Carry above the cap bleeds at 8 m/s² (SPEC-91).
- **Grapple and gravity:** the grapple tether adds 40 m/s² of cornering grip within a pylon's reach. The moon adds GM/r².
- **Not modelled:** combat, mines, doors and the moon's speed gain on the dive.
- **The Dart racer** should run about 10% faster on a clean line.

## Recommendation

**A, Derelict Run.** Reasons, in order:
1. **It shows off the voxel scale.** Players fly through a 1 km wreck and shoot it apart while racing. Nothing else shows what the engine can do as clearly in a co-op session.
2. **Its new systems are the ones exploration needs most:**
   - moving structures (cranes and rails);
   - mechanisms (doors, switches, bulkheads);
   - the tether (salvage towing).

   Derelicts are the heart of the game's mystery.
3. **Every route has a different kind of risk** (none, turrets, being trapped), and lap 2 changes the balance.

**B is the strongest second choice.** It is the most readable at a glance and the most spectacular. But it needs the portal ADR (portals move a root transform), and it says less about voxels.
