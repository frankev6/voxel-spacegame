What we took from Starsector

Notes from the wiki's ship, faction, refit and hull-mod pages and a study of the ship sprites (looked at, not stored). Our own words throughout.

  1. Tech tiers are philosophies, not upgrades. Low tech means armour, ballistics and long fights at low speed. Midline mixes everything, leans on missiles and carriers, and has the best logistics. High tech means shields, energy weapons and speed, for short hit-and-run fights. Each tier wins some matchups and loses others.
  2. Factions reuse hulls and make them their own. The Hound appears as a pirate, Luddic Path, Luddic Church and Hegemony auxiliary version. The outline barely changes; the paint, a few bolt-ons and the built-in mods do.
  3. Civilian hulls go to war. The Colossus, Atlas, Buffalo and Prometheus all have war conversions: holds become hangars or gun trusses, and the price is cargo, hull strength and often reliability. The description always explains who did it and why.
  4. Pirate refits trade hull for speed; military refits do the opposite. The pirate Falcon gets hacked engines (faster, higher burn) and gives up some defences and range. The XIV Battlegroup and Lion's Guard versions get more armour or better gear on the same hull, and the Hegemony's auxiliaries are civilian hulls brought up to military standard.
  5. Asymmetry follows use. Civilian and low-tech hulls carry offset tanks, cranes, bridges and flight decks (Tarsus, Prometheus, Mora, Condor, Astral), and high-tech hulls like the Odyssey and Aurora are asymmetric too. Warships are mostly symmetric with small offsets. Refits add asymmetry because they are bolted on.
  6. Every hull has a niche and a counter. Frigates harass, finish and capture; carriers fight from range; phase ships ambush; a capital without escorts is a target. Bigger is not better, just different.
  7. Redacted ships break the rules. Remnant drones, Omega's crystalline shards, the Threat's ribbed shells, the Dweller's red void and the Domain's derelicts each have their own geometry, unique weapons and sparse codex text. They are encounters and mysteries, not factions you trade with.
  8. Codex depth. Every hull has a designation, a maker, a short in-world story, built-in hull mods, a ship system and known variants. Each card below uses that format.

Strangers and natives. The Khuchad, the Alliance and the Vestari are the galaxy's own clans; the humans of the ARK are the newcomers. So the human ships are deliberately the odd ones out: plainer, foreign, and the only ones built to take other people's parts.

Matchups. Khuchad beat the Alliance up close (armour and rams against average shields). The Alliance beats the Vestari with numbers and missiles (shields overload and there are too few Vestari to replace losses). The Vestari beat the Khuchad by never letting them close. Frigates matter everywhere: a CORSAC pack on an ARGALI's open side, an AUDITOR's harpoon on a fleeing EDICT.

ARK TECH ARK expeditionThe humans · strangers in this galaxy · the player's faction

Your lore. The A.R.K. expedition was supposed to save humanity, and a long time passed before it arrived. Earlier Arks went ahead and were lost; their wrecks and beacons turn up across the galaxy. Behind the expedition there is nothing: only the Silence.

Doctrine. Nothing local and nothing wasted. Every ARK ship is an Earth pattern printed by the ARK's fabricators and built to be repaired by its own crew from whatever they can find. They are few, far from home and outgunned by every clan: no native shields, no energy weapons worth the name. So every hull has one job the expedition can't afford to lose (find the way, feed the fleet, break through, rebuild), and every hull has adapter points for the alien parts its crew takes. They are built from the same standard blocks the player uses: the most legible, most modifiable hulls in the galaxy.

Paint. Bone and graphite plates, safety-yellow drop tanks and fabricators, hazard stripes on every edge that can hit something, red and green navigation lights and stencilled pattern numbers. Next to the clans they look plain, practical and foreign.

Built into every hull. Printed pattern (the ARK can rebuild any of them from scratch) · Crew-repairable · Adapter points (clan parts can be bolted on)   Mods they make. Field refits with clan parts (gone native), and wrecks of earlier Ark patterns to recover. Both are in the refit bay.

LOW TECH Khuchad ClanLow tech · bulky · pack and chief

Your lore. A warfare tradition. Hegemonic and grabby: they take a world, strip what is easy and move on. Low-to-midline tech, in large numbers of small ships with one large ship leading the pack.

Doctrine. Pack and chief. Cheap, thick-skinned frigates hunt in packs of six to twelve around one huge chief-ship that is also the clan's moving camp. Every hull is built like a mounted warrior: heavy armour on the port side (the shield arm), the ram or main gun offset to starboard (the lance arm). They circle anticlockwise to keep the armour toward the enemy, then turn in to strike. Ballistic guns, rams and boarders; weak shields; slow.

Paint. Rust hulls, soot lamellar armour, ember kill-tallies on the shield side. Trophy plates cut from beaten enemies are welded on in the enemy's own colours.

Built into every hull. Trophy plating (armour grows with victories) · Pack link (frigates fight harder near their chief) · Forward ward (a narrow bow shield only)   Mods they make. Prize conversions (K): a captured freighter becomes a carrier or gun-barge by coring its holds and welding soot slabs over one side.

MIDLINE Triple Alliance (Tlaxcala)Midline · hybrid · standard spines, swappable modules

Your lore. A conglomerate of three big players that behave almost like megacorporations, holding an oligopoly known as the Triple Alliance.

Doctrine. Every hull is a spine in one of three sizes with standard module bays, so the same hull leaves the yard as a freighter, a carrier or a gunship depending on the contract. Ships are hybrids by default: they carry cargo and enough guns to keep it. A ship's outline tells you its contract. Mixed weapons, missiles and drones; average armour and shields; the best logistics in the sector. The three houses (names proposed): POCHTECA FREIGHT (trade and logistics), ITZTLI ARMS (security and weapons) and CAISSON HEAVY INDUSTRIES (mining and salvage: the batch-2 line).

Paint. Ivory spines and bridges, corporate-green module bays, mint running lights. A house band marks the builder: gold for Pochteca, obsidian for Itztli, hazard chevrons for Caisson.

Built into every hull. Standard bays (modules swap at any Alliance port) · Corporate insurance (cheap to repair, expensive to lose)   Mods they make. Itztli security refits (I): armour and guns on civilian hulls, including the Q-ship, a freighter that hides its guns in containers.

HIGH TECH VestariHigh tech · slick · few hands, long lives

Your lore. Technologically advanced, dogmatic and democratic. Xenophobic: peaceful as long as no foreigner enters their space. They believe they are the direct descendants of the Aksharans and that all other life is lesser. High-tech ships in small fleets; a century-long gestation means there are very few of them.

Doctrine. With so few people, every ship is an heirloom built to last centuries and never to lose its crew: strong shields, energy weapons, speed, heavy automation and very small crews. They don't trade blows; they choose where the fight happens and leave when it turns. Every hull carries one offset fin or spire, the speaker's station, where the elected captain stands.

Paint. Pearl planes and azure keels with ice-blue light-lines in the seams. No weathering and no markings: everyone already knows whose ship it is.

Built into every hull. Heirloom frame (repairs itself between fights) · Few hands (lose a few crew and the ship is crippled)   Mods they make. None. The Vestari never refit a hull; the single exception is a scandal (the Stolen CRADLE, in the refit bay).

OUTLAW ScavengersOutlaw · no yards, only refits

Your lore. Pirates loyal to the Scavenger King (named in the design system's faction colours).

Doctrine. Every Scavenger ship was someone else's first. They strip armour and cargo for speed, bolt on salvaged engines, put guns on open trusses where the holds were, and patch everything in primer. Faster, weaker and never the same twice. Their refits are in the refit bay below; this is the King's own ship.

Paint. Primer grey, oxide-red slashes, mismatched yellow panels, with the victim's old paint still showing through.

Built into every hull. Gutted holds (+speed, −hull) · Hot engines (salvaged engines run past their limit) · Mismatched parts   Mods they make. (S) refits of anything they can catch.

Rammers

Ramming is a game system: ram damage comes from collision impulse (mass × speed), and with voxel destruction both ships take the hit. So a rammer needs its own build: mass up front in a solid, replaceable prow; a spine that carries the shock so the rammer doesn't snap; lots of thrust, plus reverse thrust to pull free; and nothing fragile near the bow. Its counter is anything that turns faster or keeps its distance. Every card now has a Ram bar.

Who rams: the Khuchad live for it (TARPAN, the ARGALI's breaching ram, the KULAN's bow). The ARK learned it out of necessity (ICEBREAKER). Scavengers do it once per ship (the FIRESHIP). The Alliance would rather harpoon you and tow you in. The Vestari never ram: with so few of them, trading a hull and its crew for one hit is unthinkable. Batch 2 also has rammers: PENITENT (NOCTURNE frigate) and the MAUSOLEUM's ram.

Refit bay: cargo hulls re-specced for war

The same hull refitted by different people for different reasons, like a Falcon and its pirate version. Each refit keeps the base outline so you can still recognise the hull, then changes it where the new job needs it. The stat bars show what the refit gained (+) and lost (−).

In our game a refit is just a blueprint edit (cargo blocks out, gun blocks and thrusters on trusses in), so these double as examples of what players can do to hulls they capture.

Batch 2: form follows function

Every ship has one job it is best at, and its outline comes from that job: armour where it takes hits, guns where they bear, hangars, tanks, radiators and engines where the work needs them. Each line shares a doctrine, a build grammar and a paint pattern. Pick one per class.

DRYDOCK · line B · Caisson Heavy Industries

Doctrine. A deep-space mining and salvage consortium. Its ships are built around one big industrial module each (a cutter head, a tank cluster, a ring, a crucible, a rail) and armed with mining gear turned on people. They live far from ports, so they repair, refuel and refine for themselves.

Paint pattern. Graphite hulls; safety-yellow ore-armour plates on the leading edges and sponsons; black-and-yellow chevrons on every bow; white crew modules and tanks; amber work lights.

NOCTURNE · line B · the Knell choir

Doctrine. A militant mortuary order that recovers the dead from every battlefield and wages holy war with what it salvages. It fights in armour, at close range and by boarding, with storm coils and relic weapons. Its ships are laid out like the buildings it worships in: naves, crossings, apses, cloisters, towers and octagonal chapter houses, each one doing a job (a gun deck, a launch court, a vault, an engine house), with flying buttresses carrying guns outboard.

Paint pattern. Bone-ivory armour; blackened-bronze buttresses and frames; oxblood roof bands and banners; gilt trim and rose windows; candle-amber lancet windows; blue-white engine light.
